Support Privacy

Ledger keeps your hours in a record only you control, shows what the agreement actually pays for the day you worked, and reads the public federal filings your local and your pension fund have to publish.

It is free.

No account, no subscription, no ads, no trial. It was built by a laborer for the guys he works with, and it is not going to start charging you later.

Getting help

Email ledger@the-undesirables.com. Tell me what phone you have and what you were doing when it went wrong. If you use the Settings → Report a problem button inside the app, it fills that in for you — and it never attaches anything you logged.

Common questions

Where are my hours stored?

On your phone, and nowhere else. There is no server. Nothing you log is uploaded, and no one — including me — can read it. See the privacy policy.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Your log goes with it. That is the trade for having no one else hold your records. Use Settings → Back up. It writes two files: one you can read and print, and one that restores everything. Send them to yourself and keep them somewhere you will still have in a year. Do this before you need it.

Can I change an entry after I save it?

You can void it, and you can log a correction. You cannot silently rewrite it. Every entry is sealed with a hash that links to the one before it, so the log can show it has not been tampered with. A crossed-out line in a logbook is still evidence; a log that can be quietly edited is not.

The overtime split looks wrong for my day.

The app uses the rules in the published agreement, which sometimes differ from how a job actually pays. Check How this day is paid under the hours to see the rule and the article it comes from. If your job pays something different, that is worth a conversation with your steward — and email me if you think the app has the rule wrong.

Why does it ask why my day was short?

Because showing up is paid time. Article 39 sets a floor for a day cut short by weather, by being sent home, or by waiting on the site, and most people have never read it. The app tells you the floor and cites the paragraph. It never changes your hours by itself.

The microphone will not work.

Open the iPhone Settings app, scroll to Ledger, and turn on Microphone and Speech Recognition. Dictation runs on the device.

Are the salary and pension numbers real?

They come from filings unions and benefit plans are required by federal law to make public — LM-2 reports to the U.S. Department of Labor and Form 5500 reports under ERISA. The app shows what is in those filings and says which one. It does not editorialize the numbers, and anything it deduces rather than reads is labeled as a deduction.

Is this the union's app?

No. Ledger is published independently by The Undesirables LLC. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by or connected to LIUNA, any local union, district council, benefit fund, or employer. Union names appear only as they appear in the public records the app cites.

What this app is not

It is not legal or financial advice. It shows public records and the plain terms of published agreements so you can go ask better questions. Verify anything that matters with your local, your fund office, or a lawyer.

Lawsuits shown on the Radar tab are filed claims, taken from public federal dockets. A filed suit is an allegation until a court says otherwise, and every entry links to the record so you can read it yourself.
